# Voice rules (example)

The voice of the invented company Contoso. Ten rules at most, each an ID and one yes/no question about a draft. The answer `yes` means the draft follows the rule. A rule is a condition a reader can check; replace these with your team's own.

- `voice-short-sentences`: Is every sentence 25 words or fewer?
- `voice-you`: Does the draft speak to the reader as "you" instead of "the customer" or "users"?
- `voice-numbers`: Does every claim about cost, time, or size give a number, or say that the number is not known?
- `voice-no-superlatives`: Is the draft free of unsupported superlatives such as best, leading, or world-class?
- `voice-name-the-thing`: Does each paragraph name a specific product feature, page, or customer type?
- `voice-answer-first`: Does the first sentence answer the question in the page title?
- `voice-active`: Does each sentence name who does the action?
- `voice-say-gaps`: When the approved files lack a fact, does the draft say the fact is not published?
